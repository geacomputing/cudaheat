\subsection{Feature-Space Results Representation}
To investigate the temporal evolution of MHWs, a multidimensional feature-space representation was developed, combining complementary descriptors of thermal intensity, persistence, and nocturnal recovery. %Each marker represents an aggregated group of significant events, positioned according to the fraction of strong daytime threshold exceedances (horizontal axis) and the fraction of observations remaining above the climatological threshold (vertical axis). Marker size represents the fraction of nocturnal observations falling below the threshold, providing an indication of nighttime thermal recovery, while marker color identifies the temporal grouping of events by season and year.

The resulting trajectories reveal an apparent temporal evolution in MHW characteristics. Earlier events tend to exhibit weaker daytime exceedances, greater fragmentation, and more pronounced nocturnal recovery. Conversely, recent events progressively occupy the upper-right portion of the feature space, suggesting increasingly strong daytime exceedances and sustained thermal anomalies. The simultaneous reduction in marker size further suggests a transition from events exhibiting substantial nocturnal recovery toward conditions characterized by limited nighttime relief and greater thermal persistence.

These patterns suggest a progressive intensification of MHW characteristics, accompanied by reduced fragmentation and increasingly persistent nighttime warming. Importantly, the multidimensional representation allows these complementary aspects of event evolution to be examined simultaneously, revealing relationships that may remain obscured when individual metrics are considered separately.

To support this analysis, a dedicated interactive visualization environment was developed, enabling dynamic adjustment of metric combinations, temporal aggregation, and graphical parameters. This facilitates systematic exploration of the feature space and identification of emerging patterns. Further statistical assessment will be necessary to establish the robustness and significance of the observed temporal evolution.

%At event scale, \code{event_timeseries()} returns a padded time window containing SST, climatology, threshold, raw exceedance and event identifiers. These fields allow onset, interruption and termination to be inspected against the diurnally resolved reference state. At point scale, \code{point_timeseries()} and \code{point_events()} provide the corresponding local history and catalogue subset. Together, these products support case studies in which the event boundary and the derived taxonomy can be examined before they are aggregated spatially or temporally.

%For regional analyses, the sparse catalogue can be filtered by date, duration or point index and reconstructed lazily only where a dense map is needed. The recommended reporting unit is therefore not solely an event count: it is the combination of source product, hourly climatology definition, detection configuration and versioned enrichment rules. This combination provides the context required to compare case studies without conflating differences in scientific definition with differences in observed thermal behaviour.
